Visualizing Tropospheric Temperature Gradients & El Niño Wind Dynamics

Processing atmospheric wind vector fields with Python and Matplotlib to build an interactive web animation demonstrating how El Niño alters Tropospheric Temperature Gradients and Indian monsoon circulation.

Author: Diyan Maner
Supervisor: Dr. Supriyo Chakraborty
Affiliation: Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM)
Period: Jun 2026 – Aug 2026

Publication: Research outcomes and visualization were accepted and published in Ocean Digest, the quarterly publication of the Ocean Society of India. Read Publication (PDF) ↗

Scientific Context: TTG & El Niño

The Tropospheric Temperature Gradient (TTG)—denoted as $\Delta\text{TT}$—is a primary thermodynamic engine of the Indian Summer Monsoon. It represents the temperature contrast between the upper troposphere over the northern landmass (South Asia and the Tibetan Plateau, ~5°N–35°N, 40°E–100°E) and the southern equatorial Indian Ocean (~15°S–5°N, 40°E–100°E), typically averaged across the 600 hPa to 200 hPa vertical layer.

Under normal monsoon conditions, strong northern heating produces a steep positive meridional gradient, pulling moisture-laden low-level southwesterly winds (the Findlater jet) across the Arabian Sea into India.

During El Niño phases (anomalous sea surface warming in the equatorial Pacific, Niño 3.4 region), the atmospheric Walker circulation shifts eastward. This induces anomalous upper-tropospheric subsidence and anomalous thermal warming patterns over the tropics, diminishing the meridional temperature gradient. The weakened TTG slackens low-level southwesterly winds and suppresses convective rainfall, often resulting in severe monsoon deficits.

Data Sources & Processing Pipeline

To model and communicate these circulation changes clearly, the project bridged scientific data processing with web visualization:

  • Atmospheric Datasets: Analyzed gridded climate reanalysis datasets containing vertical tropospheric temperature profiles ($T$) and horizontal wind components—zonal ($u$) and meridional ($v$) vectors—at critical pressure levels (850 hPa low-level flow and 200 hPa upper-level easterly jet).
  • Vector Processing in Python & Matplotlib: Extracted $u$ and $v$ wind velocity matrices, computed wind speed, divergence, and direction angles, and generated vector streamplots and quiver representations using Python's scientific stack (`NumPy`, `Matplotlib`).
  • Coordinate Extraction: Converted continuous vector fields and isobar contours into optimized JSON coordinate trajectories and animation frame sequences.

Frontend Web Animation

Atmospheric scientists and journal readers needed to inspect dynamic wind direction shifts without installing scientific Python libraries or GIS tools. I developed an interactive in-browser animation:

  • Dynamic Vector Field: Animated wind direction flow lines across the Indian Ocean and subcontinent to illustrate wind deceleration and direction deflection during El Niño years.
  • Direct Integration: Deployed as a standalone lightweight web animation hosted directly on the Ocean Society of India portal to accompany the published journal issue.

Outcomes

  • Published in Ocean Digest: The methodology, findings, and animation were accepted and published by the Ocean Society of India in the June 2026 issue.
  • Interactive Accessibility: Replaced static, hard-to-interpret 2D wind arrows with an intuitive web animation that illustrates atmospheric responses to El Niño.
